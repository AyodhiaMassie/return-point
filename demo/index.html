The art of picking up

01 / A SMALL BEGINNING

Give your attention a place to land.

Every good reading session begins with a little space. A clear page, a useful question, and the willingness to follow an idea for a while. You do not have to finish the whole thing to find something worthwhile.

Sometimes the first paragraph gives you exactly what you need. Sometimes the real insight is waiting much further down. An article is a conversation you can enter at your own pace, with enough time to notice what matters.

Try scrolling slowly through this page. The numbered chapters are landmarks. When you save your place, ReturnPoint remembers a proportion of the document rather than assuming that every future version will have exactly the same height.

02 / FOLLOW THE THREAD

Curiosity is a useful compass.

A question makes reading active. Why does this approach work? Where could the reasoning fail? What would happen if one assumption changed? Questions give an idea somewhere to go when you have finished the paragraph.

Good documentation rewards that kind of attention. An example becomes a small experiment. A diagram becomes a model you can test. A paragraph becomes a new way to describe a problem you have seen before.

You might save a note that says, “Come back to the example in the next chapter.” A personal reminder is often more useful than a perfect summary. It helps the next reading session begin without having to reconstruct your whole train of thought.

03 / MAKE A LITTLE NOTE

Leave a thought for your future self.

Your note does not need to be polished. A half-formed question, a surprising sentence in your own words, or a connection to another project can be enough. Its purpose is to make returning easier.

There is a difference between storing information and preserving context. A URL tells you where a page lives. A reading position tells you where you stopped. A note tells you why you wanted to come back.

Try the note editor with multiple lines, punctuation, or a piece of code. ReturnPoint displays these as ordinary text. The extension never treats a note as a command or as markup to execute.

04 / TAKE YOUR TIME

Progress can be quiet.

Not every page is something to rush through. A long explanation may need more than one sitting. Returning to it after a break can reveal connections that were hard to see when everything was new.

This is a useful point to save a first ReturnPoint. Open the side panel using the toolbar icon. Choose Save Reading Position, grant access to this local website, add a note, and save. The library will show the progress you just captured.

Scroll a little further and save the same URL again. You should still have only one entry. Its progress and note should change, while its identity stays the same. Saving is always a deliberate action.

05 / THE HALFWAY LANDMARK

You have found a good place to pause.

This chapter is the middle landmark for the acceptance test. Save a note such as, “Paused near chapter five; next time read about returning.” Remember which heading is in view, then close the tab.

For the persistence test, close your browser completely and reopen it. Keep the local Vite server running in your terminal. Open ReturnPoint from its toolbar icon and find the saved entry in Active.

Click Resume exactly once. A new tab should open this article, the side panel should remain available, and the webpage should scroll back to approximately this part of the document. Your full note should appear beside it.

06 / RETURNING

A familiar place makes starting easier.

The first few moments of a return matter. A recognizable heading and a small reminder remove the friction of finding your place again. You can spend your attention on the idea instead of on the search.

Webpages can change between visits. A different window height or new content can move a heading. Saving the relative progress gives a useful approximation for ordinary articles, while a few bounded retries let normal layout changes settle.

If you resumed here successfully, read another chapter and click Update Progress in the side panel. It should capture your new position only at that moment. Moving to another URL will disable the update action until you select the matching page.

07 / LOCAL BY DESIGN

Your reading belongs to you.

A small utility can do its job without an account. ReturnPoint keeps your URLs, notes, and saved positions in this browser profile. It does not send them to a service or synchronize them to another device.

Website access is granted only when you choose to save or resume on that website. A grant lets the extension read and restore the document’s scroll position there. The extension does not install a persistent content script that watches your reading.

You can inspect or revoke access through Chrome’s extension settings. If access has been removed, the next save or resume will offer to request it again. Declining leaves your saved entries intact.

08 / FINISHING

Let a finished page become a record.

When an article has given you what you need, mark it completed. It moves out of the active list and into Completed, where its note is still available. Completion is a change of state, not a deletion.

You may want to return later with a new question. Move the entry back to Active and resume it again. The same saved identity remains, and the library sorts entries by when you most recently changed them.

Try this sequence now: mark your demo ReturnPoint completed, view the Completed section, and move it back to Active. These simple transitions are useful checks that local storage and the interface stay in agreement.

09 / KEEP WHAT MATTERS

A small library can stay useful.

A reading library works best when its entries have a reason to exist. A good note can preserve that reason. A completed entry can hold onto a useful conclusion. An unneeded entry can be removed when its work is done.

Choose Delete on the demo entry to see the confirmation. Keep it first, then try again and confirm the deletion. The entry and its note should disappear from the local library.

This demo intentionally uses a single ordinary document that scrolls in the window. It does not use an infinite feed, an embedded PDF viewer, or a nested reading container. Those need different approaches and are outside this extension’s MVP.

10 / ANOTHER BEGINNING

There is always something worth returning to.

You have reached the last chapter. If you save here, progress should be close to the bottom. If you resize the browser and resume, the target should adjust to the current document height.

You can also save at the very top of the page. A progress of zero is a valid position. Very short HTML pages can be saved too; they simply resume at the top and the panel explains that the page fits on screen.

That completes the local demo. Use an ordinary long article or documentation page for a second test, granting access to that individual website when prompted. A small break should be all that separates you from your next good idea.

You’re all caught up.
